If you do use one, use it in this order

Five numbered steps: try the problem yourself first, go back to the course material, ask course staff, only then ask an LLM for clarification, and when you do prompt it be specific, go step by step and verify every answer. A line underneath warns that its answers may be wrong or may take an approach this course has not taught.

  1. Try it yourself first — trace the code, draw the picture, run the debugger.
  2. Go back to the course material.
  3. Ask us: office hours, lab, lecture, Piazza.
  4. Only then ask an LLM, and ask it clarifying questions.
  5. Be clear and specific, go step by step, and verify what comes back.

Its answers may be wrong, and may solve the problem in a way this course has not taught you — which leaves you more to untangle, not less.

The order is the content. Steps 1 to 3 are where the learning is; step 4 is a tool you reach for once you already know what you are stuck on. Point out that steps 2 and 3 cost nothing and are faster than they think — a UTA in lab looks at their actual project, which no chatbot does.
